LadybugDB vs ArcadeDB

Specialist vs. generalist. LadybugDB does one thing brilliantly — embedded columnar graph analytics in Cypher (MIT). ArcadeDB (Apache 2.0, OrientDB lineage) does many things well — documents, graphs, key-value, and vectors behind SQL/Gremlin/Cypher/GraphQL.

TL;DR verdict

Choose LadybugDB for the fastest embedded graph analytics, Cypher focus, Kuzu compatibility, and agent-ready MCP/OpenAPI. Choose ArcadeDB when you need one engine for mixed document + graph + vector workloads, JVM embedding, or HA server replication — and accept a broader, less specialized engine.

The specialist advantage

Multi-model sounds convenient — until your analytical query scans 100M edges and the general-purpose store can't compress or vectorize like a columnar engine. LadybugDB bets that graph analytics deserve their own storage layout, exactly as DuckDB proved for tables. If your workload is graph-heavy (fraud rings, recommendations, knowledge graphs), the specialist wins on speed and simplicity.

When ArcadeDB is the better fit

  • You genuinely need documents + graphs + vectors in one transaction (e.g., CMS with knowledge graph).
  • Your platform is JVM-first and you want Java-embedded deployment with HA failover.
  • Your team already speaks Gremlin or ArcadeDB SQL and wants polyglot querying.

The JVM tax — worse when it's "embedded"

ArcadeDB inherits not just OrientDB's record-store design but its runtime: the JVM. That means heap sizing before you store a byte, GC pauses in the tail of every traversal, sluggish startup, per-object header overhead on every vertex and edge, and a hundreds-of-MB runtime tax on anything claiming to be "embedded." True embedding means disappearing into the host process — a native C++ library like LadybugDB, or a Rust binary like HelixDB: deterministic latency, tiny footprint, no runtime to tune. LadybugDB goes further: plain C++ means it runs on a range of devices — WASM in the browser, native iOS and Android, and any architecture with a C compiler — so the same engine powers web demos, mobile apps, and edge deployments. Shipping a JVM inside your edge agent or per-tenant sidecar to run a graph is the opposite of embedded; it's a server you smuggled into the process.

Ecosystem edge: Icebug, icebug-format, and NetworkX go columnar

A generalist engine gives you a little of every algorithm library and the best of none. The specialist brings an ecosystem:

  • Icebug: a columnar fork of NetworKit with 220+ graph algorithms running natively on LadybugDB's CSR data — depth no multi-model grab-bag can match.
  • icebug-format: open Parquet-with-CSR storage keeps graphs in the lakehouse (GraphLake) and readable by all Parquet/Arrow tooling — versus a proprietary multi-model store.
  • NetworkX goes columnar too: the nx-arrow and nx-ladybug backends bring columnar scale to Python's most popular graph library.

Migration notes

ArcadeDB's OpenCypher support means MATCH queries largely transfer. Export vertices/edges as CSV/JSON, define NODE TABLE / REL TABLE schemas in LadybugDB, and COPY FROM. Document collections that aren't graph-shaped can stay in a document store — LadybugDB pairs well alongside one.
